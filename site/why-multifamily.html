Valeward  /  Why multifamily

The case for multifamily

An assessment of the asset class: the mechanics by which returns are generated, the conditions supporting the current opportunity, and the four principal ways in which an investment of this type loses money.

Demand characteristics

Most commercial property types depend on a discretionary decision. An office requires an occupier to elect to lease space; a retail centre requires consumers to elect to shop there. Residential demand derives from a non-discretionary requirement.

This underpins the thesis. Apartment communities have historically demonstrated lower income volatility through recessionary periods than most other commercial real estate. Leases reset annually, allowing rents to track inflation rather than remaining fixed below it for an extended term.

None of this constitutes a guarantee. Occupancy declines, operating expenses rise and submarkets over-build. The distinction is that the underlying demand is closer to a necessity than that supporting any other institutional property type.

Return drivers

An understanding of these mechanics clarifies why an identical asset may represent either a sound investment or a permanent loss, determined principally by the basis and structure at acquisition.

01

Cash flow

Rental income less operating costs and debt service. The residual is distributed. This is the only driver observable quarterly, and consequently the one that dominates investor perception of an asset.

02

Principal paydown

Rental income services the loan, and amortization converts a meaningful proportion of debt into equity over a five-year hold without further capital contribution.

03

Forced appreciation

Commercial property is valued on income. An additional $100 of monthly rent across 250 units represents approximately $300,000 of annual income and, at a 5.5% capitalization rate, some $5.4M of value.

04

Tax treatment

Depreciation, accelerated through cost segregation, produces passive losses reported on a Schedule K‑1, frequently offsetting distributions received in the same period.

Three conditions supporting the current opportunity

The supply cycle is turning

The 2022–2024 construction cycle is delivering into 2025–2026, and starts have declined materially since. Assets acquired now are generally held through the period in which new deliveries recede.

Ownership is transferring

Sponsors that employed floating-rate debt in 2021 face maturities they are unable to refinance at the original basis. This produces motivated vendors for acquirers whose own capital structures remain intact.

Household formation favours rental demand

Elevated mortgage rates and residential prices extend the period for which prospective purchasers remain renters. This is adverse for those households and supportive of apartment demand; we state the mechanism directly rather than obscure it.
